# Motion A — The Cold Call

For businesses with **no website**, where you have already built them one.

> Use `03-Sales-Playbook.md` for Motion B (businesses that have a bad website).
> Different situation, different psychology, different script.

---

## Before you dial

1. **Open their generated site and read it.** Two minutes. If anything is wrong —
   a mangled business name, the wrong industry — fix it before calling. One bad
   site burns the whole call.
2. **Have the link ready to text.** The call is worthless if you cannot get the
   site in front of them within sixty seconds.
3. **Call between 9–11am or 2–4pm on Tuesday to Thursday.** Mondays are chaos,
   Fridays they have checked out, and trades are on site early and late.
4. **Stand up.** It changes your voice more than you would expect.

---

## Part 1 — The opener

> **"Hey [Owner Name], I was looking for a plumber in Bentonville and found you,
> but I noticed you don't have a website… so I went ahead and built you one.
> Would you like to see it?"**

That is the whole opener. Say it warmly, then **stop talking.**

The pause is what pulls them in. Most people cannot resist finding out what you
built. Whoever speaks first after that question loses the frame — so don't.

**Do not** add "is now a good time?", "I'll be quick", or "I hope I'm not
interrupting". Every one of those is a signal that a pitch is coming.

---

## Part 2 — Foot in the door

**"Who is this? How did you get my number?"**

> "Totally fair. I found [Business Name] on Google while I was looking up plumbers
> in [City]. Your number's public on your listing — that's all. I do this for a
> handful of local businesses."

Say it flatly and without apology. You did nothing wrong, and sounding defensive
makes it sound like you did.

**"What's the catch?"**

> "Honestly, no catch on the website. I build them because it's way easier to show
> a business owner what I can do than to talk at them about it. You look at it — if
> you love it, we can talk. If not, no hard feelings."

**"I'm not interested."**

> "No problem at all. Can I text you the link anyway? Costs you nothing and it's
> already built. If you never open it, that's fine."

**Roughly a third will say yes to that.** The site does the selling later.

**"I'm busy / I'm on a job."**

> "Understood — I'll text you the link and get out of your hair. Have a look
> tonight if you get a minute."

Then actually get off the phone. Respecting that earns you the callback.

---

## Part 3 — The reveal

Once they agree to look:

> "Great — texting it now. It'll take about ten seconds to load."

**Then be quiet while they open it.** Let them react first.

When they do speak, whatever they say:

> "That's your actual Google rating and review count on there, by the way — 4.6
> from 116 reviews. And your real phone number, so every button on it calls you."

**Then the line that separates you from everyone else who has ever cold-called
them:**

> "One other thing — it's built so ChatGPT and Google's AI can actually read it.
> Right now if someone asks ChatGPT for a plumber in Bentonville, you're not in
> the answer because there's nothing for it to read. This fixes that."

Most local businesses have never heard this from anyone. It is true, it is
verifiable in thirty seconds, and it is the reason they will remember your call.

---

## Part 4 — The price

Wait for them to ask. They almost always will. If they don't after a minute or
two of positive reaction:

> "So — do you want to know what it costs to keep it?"

Then:

> "It's $297 a month. That covers the site being live and hosted, an AI chat
> widget that answers questions and books jobs, a voice AI that picks up when
> you can't, automatic texts when you miss a call, review requests going out after
> every job, and a simple CRM so you're not running leads off scraps of paper.
>
> No contract. If you want to stop, you stop."

**Say the number and stop talking.** Do not justify it. Do not stack more
features. The silence after a price is uncomfortable and you must let them fill it.

---

## Part 5 — Objections

**"That's too expensive."**
> "What were you expecting?" — then wait. Usually they name something like $50.
> "That gets you a page that sits there. This one answers your phone when you're
> under a sink. Genuinely, if one extra job a month covers it — what's an average
> job worth to you?"
>
> Let them do the arithmetic out loud. For a plumber a single call-out often
> covers the month.

**"I don't need a website, I get all my work from word of mouth."**
> "That's the best kind of work. Here's the thing though — when someone recommends
> you, the first thing that person does is search your name. Right now they find a
> Google listing and nothing else. Half of them call whoever *does* have a site.
> This just makes sure the referrals you already earn actually reach you."

**"I already have a Facebook page."**
> "That's better than nothing. Two problems: you don't own it, and it doesn't show
> up when someone searches 'plumber near me' at 9pm with a leak. This does both."

**"Let me think about it."**
> "Of course. The site stays up either way for a couple of weeks, so have a proper
> look. What's the bit you want to think about — the money, or whether it'll
> actually bring work in?"
>
> Never accept the surface answer. Those two objections have completely different
> responses, and you cannot handle one you have not identified.

**"Can you just build it and I pay once?"**
> "I can, but I'd be doing you a disservice. The site is maybe a third of the
> value — the rest is the phone being answered and the reviews coming in, and
> that's monthly by nature. If you genuinely only want the site, it's $1,200
> one-time and it's yours, no support."
>
> Some will take this. Take the money and revisit in three months.

**"I need to talk to my wife / partner."**
> "Makes sense. Send them the link — it'll explain itself faster than I can.
> I'll follow up Thursday?"
>
> Always name a specific day. "I'll follow up sometime" never happens.

---

## Part 6 — The close

> "Alright — easiest thing is I get it live under your own name today, and you'll
> see it working before the first payment comes out. I just need about ten minutes
> to get your details. Want to do that now, or shall I call you back at four?"

**Two options, both yes.** Never ask "so do you want to go ahead?" — that is a
question with a free no attached.

---

## If it goes to voicemail

> "Hi, this is Cassey. I was looking for a plumber in Bentonville, found your
> listing, and noticed you don't have a website — so I built you one to show you
> what it'd look like. I'll text you the link. No cost, nothing to sign up for.
> Have a look when you get a minute."

**Then text immediately:**

> Hi [Name] — Cassey here, just left you a voicemail. Built a website for
> [Business] to show you what it could look like: [link]
> No charge, no catch. Have a look when you get a chance.

**The voicemail plus text combination outperforms the live call** for trades, who
are usually holding a tool when you ring.

---

## Follow-up

**Day 3:** *"Hi [Name] — did the site come through OK? Happy to change anything on it."*

**Day 7:** *"Hi [Name] — I'll take the demo down at the end of the week to free up space. Want me to leave it up?"*

That second one is honest scarcity — you genuinely will not maintain fifty demo
sites forever — and it produces more replies than any other message in the sequence.

**Then stop.** Two follow-ups. A third costs you the referral.

## Track only this

| | Count |
|---|---|
| Dials | |
| Conversations (they spoke) | |
| Agreed to see the site | |
| Actually opened it | |
| Closed | |

**"Agreed to see the site" is your key number.** If it is under 40% of
conversations, the opener is wrong — change one thing and test another twenty-five
calls. Everything downstream depends on it.

---

## The rule

**Never call about a site you have not opened and read yourself.** The whole
approach rests on them seeing something genuinely good in the first ten seconds.
One embarrassing site and you have taught yourself the pitch doesn't work, when
actually you just skipped two minutes of checking.
