# WebGenie Sales Playbook

**Target for weeks 3–4:** 30 audits sent → 12 replies → 5 calls → 2 blueprints → 1 build.

---

## The core principle

Everyone in this market sells the same way: they ask for a call, then try to create urgency on
the call. You are going to do the opposite. **You will do the work first, hand it over free, and
ask for nothing.** That single inversion is your entire competitive advantage, because it is the
only approach that survives a cold prospect's scepticism.

The report creates the problem. Your Loom creates the trust. The call only closes what those two
already did. If you find yourself persuading on a call, the report was not specific enough.

---

## Step 1 — Build the list

Pick **one** niche. Dentists recommended (see `00-START-HERE.md` for why).

Find 30 businesses where:

- The site looks five or more years old, or is on Wix/Squarespace/GoDaddy with a stock template
- They are clearly spending money on ads (they have budget, and they are wasting it)
- They have 50+ Google reviews (they have volume; they are not a startup)
- They are within 30 minutes of you (local means you can offer to visit, and that closes)

**Where to find them:** Google Maps for "cosmetic dentist [city]", then sort by review count.
Businesses with many reviews and a bad site are your ideal customer — they have demand and are
leaking it.

**Disqualify immediately:** anyone whose site is already excellent, anyone with fewer than 20
reviews, and any national chain. You cannot help the first, the second cannot afford you, and
the third has an agency already.

---

## Step 2 — Run the audit *before* you contact them

This is the part everyone gets wrong. Do not ask permission. Do not send a "would you be
interested" email. **Do the work, then send it.**

Ten audits takes an afternoon. The response rate difference between "can I audit your site?"
and "I audited your site, here it is" is roughly five to one.

For each: run the engine, fill in `01-Client-Audit-Report.html`, save as
`Audit-BusinessName-2026-08.html`, and check it reads like a human wrote it.

**Quality bar before you send:** every finding names a real number from their real site. If a
finding could be copy-pasted onto any other website, delete it. Generic findings destroy the
credibility that specific ones build.

## Step 3 — The outreach message

### Email — the primary version

> **Subject:** Your booking form (found something)
>
> Hi Dr. [Name],
>
> I analyse dental websites and I ran one on [practice] this morning — unprompted, no charge,
> nothing attached to it.
>
> One thing stood out. Your booking form asks for eleven pieces of information, including
> insurance member ID, before it will accept an appointment. On a phone, your call bar also
> covers the submit button — so on many devices the form cannot be completed at all.
>
> Given 68% of your visitors are on phones, that is likely costing you several consultations a
> month.
>
> Full report attached. It covers ten other things and compares you to three practices nearby.
> It is yours regardless — I am not asking for a call.
>
> If it is useful, reply and tell me. If it is not, tell me that too, it helps me.
>
> [Your name]
> [Agency] · [phone]

**Why this works:**

- The subject line is a curiosity gap about *their* asset, not a pitch
- The first sentence disarms the "what is this selling me" reflex
- The specific finding proves you actually looked, in one sentence
- "Costing you several consultations a month" converts a technical fact into money
- "I am not asking for a call" removes the reason to delete
- Asking for a reply either way makes responding easy, including a negative

**Never** open with "I hope this email finds you well", "I came across your website", or "quick
question". Those three phrases are the universal signal of a mass email and get you deleted
before line two.

### Follow-up — send 4 days later, only once

> **Subject:** Re: Your booking form
>
> Hi Dr. [Name] — just checking the report reached you, attachments sometimes get filtered.
>
> No reply needed if it is not relevant. If you would like me to walk through the three critical
> items, I will record a five-minute video, no call required.
>
> [Your name]

**One follow-up. Then stop.** A third message converts almost nobody and costs you the referral
if they ever change their mind.

### LinkedIn / DM — shorter

> Hi [Name] — I ran a free analysis on [practice]'s website this morning. Found three things
> that are likely costing you consultations, the biggest being that your booking form can't be
> submitted on most phones. Happy to send the report over, no charge and nothing attached. Want it?

### Voicemail — if you call

> Hi, this is [name] from [agency]. I'm not selling anything — I ran a free analysis on your
> website this morning and found a problem with your booking form on mobile phones. I've emailed
> the report over. Worth a look, it's about two minutes. That's it, no need to call back.

## Step 4 — The Loom walkthrough

Record this for anyone who replies, and for the top five prospects regardless. **Five minutes
maximum.** Screen-share their actual website, not your report.

**Script skeleton:**

**0:00–0:20 — Who you are, why no pitch is coming.**
> "Hi Dr. Chen, this is Cassey. Quick walkthrough of what I found on your site — about five
> minutes. I'm not going to pitch you anything at the end, this is just the report explained."

**0:20–1:30 — Show the worst problem live, on their site.**
Open their booking form on a phone-width browser window. Try to submit it. Let them watch it
fail.
> "This is your booking page at phone width. I'm going to fill it in... and here's the thing —
> the submit button is behind your call bar. I physically can't book an appointment. This is
> what about two-thirds of your visitors see."

**Do not narrate the score. Show the failure.** A person watching their own form fail is worth
more than any number you could quote.

**1:30–3:00 — The second and third problem, same treatment.**
Load their homepage with throttling on so they see the 4.8 seconds. Search their practice in
ChatGPT and show that it returns a competitor.

**3:00–4:00 — The competitor contrast.**
Open the competitor doing it right. Do not editorialise; just show it.
> "Here's Tend, about fifteen minutes from you. Three fields, books in about twenty seconds."

**4:00–4:45 — What it would take.**
> "Form is a week. Speed is two days. The AI search piece is two weeks and it's the one nobody
> in your market has done yet."

**4:45–5:00 — The soft close.**
> "Report's in your inbox with everything else. If you want to talk it through I'll put twenty
> minutes aside — link's below. If you'd rather hand this to your existing developer, please do,
> the findings are correct either way."

---

## Step 5 — The call

Twenty minutes. You are not presenting. You are diagnosing.

### Structure

**Minutes 0–2 — Set the frame.**
> "I've got twenty minutes blocked. I'd rather spend most of it understanding your practice than
> talking about the report — you've already read it. Sound OK?"

**Minutes 2–8 — Ask, and shut up.**

1. "How do most new patients find you at the moment?"
2. "Roughly how many consultations do you book in a month?"
3. "What's a new cosmetic case actually worth to you, on average?"
4. "What have you tried before on the website side, and what happened?"
5. "If the site were working properly, what would change for the practice?"

**Question 3 is the one that matters.** It gives you the number you will use for the rest of the
conversation, and it comes from their mouth rather than yours.

**Minutes 8–14 — Reflect the arithmetic back.**
> "So a cosmetic case is worth about four thousand, and you're booking maybe twelve consultations
> a month. If the form is failing for two-thirds of your mobile visitors and mobile is most of
> your traffic — even recovering two consultations a month is eight thousand. Against a build
> that's a one-off three and a half."

**Let the silence sit.** Do not fill it. The prospect does the maths themselves and that
arithmetic is far more persuasive than yours.

**Minutes 14–18 — Present one option, not three.**

Do not walk them through the whole ladder. Recommend the one you actually believe fits:
> "Honestly, I'd do the full build. The blueprint on its own makes sense if you've got a
> developer, and you don't. Three and a half, four to five weeks, and the form and speed fixes
> go live in week one so you stop bleeding immediately."

**Minutes 18–20 — Close on the next physical step.**
> "If that sounds right, I'll send a proposal this afternoon and we could start Monday. Want me
> to?"

## Objection handling

**"It's too expensive."**
> "Compared to what, out of interest?" — then wait. Usually they are comparing to a $500 Wix
> template, and the honest answer is: "Then that's the right call. A template will look fine.
> It won't fix the form or the AI search piece, and those are the two things costing you
> patients. If budget is the constraint, take the blueprint at $497 and have someone implement
> it over time — I'd genuinely rather you fixed it that way than not at all."

**"I need to think about it."**
> "Of course. What specifically are you weighing up?" — never accept the surface answer. It is
> almost always price, timing, or trust, and each has a different response. Then: "Would it help
> if I sent a one-page summary of the arithmetic we just did? You'll probably want to show
> someone."

**"We just had our website redone."**
> "How long ago?" If under a year: "Then let's not rebuild it — let me quote just the three
> critical fixes. Probably a fraction of what you're imagining, and you keep the site you paid
> for." **This is a genuinely good outcome.** A $1,200 fix job that works buys you the retainer
> later, and they will tell people you talked them out of the bigger spend.

**"Can you guarantee more patients?"**
> "No, and I'd be careful with anyone who does. What I can guarantee is that your form will work
> on a phone, your site will load in under two seconds, and you'll be visible to AI search —
> because those are measurable and I'll show you the before and after. Whether that converts to
> patients depends on your market and your pricing, which aren't mine to promise."

Never guarantee revenue. The credibility you gain by refusing is worth more than the deal you
might close by promising.

**"Send me some references."**
> Early on you will not have them. Say so: "You'd be my third dental client, so I'll be straight
> with you — I don't have a dental reference yet. What I do have is this report, which you can
> verify yourself in about ten minutes. If that's not enough, I completely understand." Honesty
> here converts better than a manufactured case study.

**"Why is the audit free? What's the catch?"**
> "It takes me about fifteen minutes now that the analysis is automated, and it's the only
> reliable way to show you I know what I'm doing. Roughly one in five people I send it to end up
> working with me. The other four get a useful document. That maths works for me."

## The proposal

Send within 4 hours of the call. Short. One page.

```
[Practice name] — Website Rebuild
Prepared by [you], [date]

WHAT WE DISCUSSED
You book roughly 12 consultations a month. A cosmetic case is worth
about $4,000. Three defects are intercepting patients before they book.

WHAT WE'LL FIX
1. Booking form — 11 fields to 4, works on every phone       Week 1
2. Load speed — 4.8s to under 2s                             Week 1
3. AI search visibility — full structured data               Week 2
4. Proof placement — reviews and results above the fold      Week 2
5. Published price ranges with financing                     Week 3
Plus: complete rebuild against the blueprint in your report

TIMELINE      4 weeks from approval. Items 1 and 2 live in week 1.
INVESTMENT    $3,500. 50% to start, 50% at launch.
INCLUDED      30 days support. All source files are yours.

WHAT I NEED FROM YOU
Logo files, 6 recent photos, and 30 minutes to confirm the copy.

Approve by replying to this email and we start Monday.
```

**Why one page:** multi-page proposals get "I'll review this with my partner" and die. One page
gets read on a phone in the car park and replied to.

**Why 50/50:** it de-risks you without making them nervous. Never do 100% up front on a first
project; never do 100% on completion either.

## After the build — the retainer conversation

Wait until the site is live and you have two weeks of data. Then:

> "Quick update — the form's converting at [X]% against [Y]% before, and load time is at 1.8s.
> One thing worth mentioning: you're now visible to AI search but nobody's tracking whether it's
> working. That's the piece I'd keep an eye on monthly, along with review generation. It's $697
> a month and it's the thing that compounds. Want me to put together what that looks like?"

**The retainer is the business.** One project pays a month. One retainer pays every month.
Every single build should end with this conversation.

## Track these five numbers only

| Metric | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Audits sent | 30 in week 3 | The only input you control |
| Reply rate | 35%+ | Below 25% means your findings are too generic |
| Call booked rate | 40% of replies | Below that means the Loom is missing |
| Close rate | 40% of calls | Below that means you are pitching, not diagnosing |
| Average project value | $3,500+ | Below that means you are discounting too early |

If reply rate is low, the problem is always the specificity of the finding in the first
paragraph. Nothing else.

## Ten opening lines, one per niche

Swap the finding for whatever your engine actually returned. **Never send one you have not
verified.**

1. **Dentist** — "Your booking form asks for insurance details before it'll accept an appointment, and on a phone the submit button is behind your call bar."
2. **Roofer** — "Your emergency number isn't clickable on mobile — someone with an active leak has to memorise it and switch apps."
3. **Med spa** — "You've got 380 reviews and none of them appear until the fourth scroll, well after most visitors have already left."
4. **Landscaper** — "Your gallery is 14MB of unoptimised photos. On a phone it takes eleven seconds, so most people never see your best work."
5. **Restoration** — "You're not appearing in AI search for water damage in [city] — a competitor is, and they've got half your reviews."
6. **Tree care** — "There's no way to request a quote without calling. Three of your four competitors take the request online."
7. **Law firm** — "Your practice area pages average 140 words. The firm ranking above you averages 1,600."
8. **Chiropractor** — "New patients can't book online at all — every path leads to a phone number, and your phones are 9-to-5."
9. **Restaurant** — "Your menu is a PDF, which means Google can't read it and neither can anyone using a screen reader."
10. **Fitness studio** — "Your class timetable is an image from 2024. It's the most-visited page on your site."

---

## The one rule

**Never send a report you would not be happy to have your name on if it were forwarded to the
competitor down the road.** It will be, eventually. That is how you get the fourth client
without sending a single email.
